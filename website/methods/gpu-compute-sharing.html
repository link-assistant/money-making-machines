GPU & Compute Sharing

Rent out your idle graphics card and computer power to AI and rendering workloads. If you already own a gaming PC, it can earn while you sleep.

Passive Intermediate

Overview

The AI surge created a shortage of GPU compute. Marketplaces now let you rent your spare GPU cycles to people training models, running inference, or rendering video. You set the price (or accept the market rate), and the platform handles matching, sandboxing, and payment.

Realistic earnings: highly dependent on your hardware. A modern high-VRAM card (e.g. an RTX 4090) can earn roughly $50–$200+ per month when well-utilised; older or low-VRAM cards earn much less. Always subtract your electricity cost before counting profit.

⚠️ Read before you start: Running a GPU at full load 24/7 increases power use, heat, and wear. Check your local electricity price — in high-cost regions this can wipe out the profit. You are renting compute to strangers' workloads, so use platforms that sandbox jobs and never run untrusted code outside their client.

Step-by-step Guide

1

Check your hardware & electricity cost

This method only pays off with a reasonably capable GPU. Before anything else:

  • Identify your GPU model and VRAM (more VRAM = more rentable jobs)
  • Find your electricity price per kWh and estimate your card's power draw under load
  • Make sure your PC can stay on and online for long stretches

Manual: On Windows, open Task Manager → Performance → GPU. On Linux, run nvidia-smi.

or check it manually above
2

Choose a compute marketplace

These are the established platforms paying GPU providers in 2026:

  • Salad — salad.com — easiest for beginners; a desktop app shares your GPU while idle and pays in cash or rewards
  • Vast.ai — vast.ai — large GPU-rental marketplace; you host and set your own price (higher earning potential, more setup)
  • io.net — io.net — decentralised GPU network for AI workloads; pays in crypto
or visit the links above manually
3

Create an account & install the host client

Sign up, then install the provider/host client for your platform. Salad is the simplest — install the desktop app, sign in, and it starts earning when your PC is idle. Vast.ai requires installing its host package and registering your machine: 

# Vast.ai host setup (Linux example)
# 1. Install Docker + NVIDIA Container Toolkit first
# 2. Install the Vast host CLI
curl -sSL https://console.vast.ai/install-vastai-host.sh | bash
# 3. Register your machine (follow the printed instructions)
vastai self-test

Salad / io.net: just download the official desktop client and sign in — no command line needed.

or follow the instructions above manually
4

Set your price & enable auto-start

On Vast.ai, set a competitive hourly price (the dashboard shows what comparable GPUs charge). On Salad/io.net, the rate is set for you. In every client, enable "Launch at startup" so your machine rejoins the pool automatically after a reboot.

Tip: schedule the client to run mainly when electricity is cheaper or when you're not gaming, to keep the work genuinely passive.

or configure manually in the client
5

Monitor utilisation, temps & payouts

Check your dashboard for utilisation (how often your GPU is rented) and keep an eye on temperatures to avoid overheating. Payout methods vary: Salad pays cash/rewards, Vast.ai pays to your balance for withdrawal, and io.net pays in crypto. Withdraw once you pass the minimum threshold.
